Dear Editor,

Thank you for the invitation to submit “Asymmetric Relations Between Needs and Data Availability in Predictive Maintenance Systems” to *Symmetry*.

The manuscript develops Limited-Data Predictive Maintenance (LD-PdM) as a conceptual characterization of maintenance settings in which consequential choices coexist with persistently weak feasible condition information. It organizes the problem through four asymmetries concerning observability, failure evidence, information acquisition, and population-level planning. Operational inclusion criteria and boundary cases distinguish this framing from dataset size alone.

The paper acknowledges overlap with small-data PHM, PdM suitability assessment, usage/load-based maintenance, soft sensing, and established maintenance decision analysis. Its contribution is an applicability checklist, a five-step procedure, and a completed railway-station decision record. The hypothetical two-door comparison removes dominated policies and contrasts minimax regret with maximin incremental gain. Their different recommendations show why an inspection choice depends on its declared objective as well as its indirect evidence and condition assumptions.

The related published GPECOM paper by Bauer and Baranowski specifies a proxy-flow model and schedules predefined service categories. The present manuscript contributes a decision-regime assessment and an explicit action rationale under competing evidence interpretations. Its hypothetical two-door calculation develops that reasoning independently of the companion's numerical illustration. The GPECOM study is cited and its service-condition outputs are distinguished from physical-failure probabilities; its technical equations, figures, and numerical results are not reproduced.

The work is submitted as a conceptual contribution under the invited title. It contains no new empirical dataset or claim of demonstrated maintenance improvement.
